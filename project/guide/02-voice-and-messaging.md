# Voice and messaging

## How CHI sounds

CHI sounds like a senior stylist with a client in the chair: knowledgeable, encouraging, and specific about what to do next.

| We are | We are not | In practice |
|---|---|---|
| Expert | Clinical | Name the technology once, then say what it does for the hair. |
| Confident | Boastful | "Protects up to 450°F / 232°C." Not "the world's most incredible protection". |
| Warm | Cute | Talk to "you". No baby talk, no puns on "mane". |
| Direct | Blunt | Lead with the benefit. Keep the instruction to three steps. |

## Messaging pillars

1. **Heat with protection.** Ceramic tools and thermal care are designed to work together. Whenever a tool appears, name the protection that goes with it.
2. **Silk-smooth results.** Shine, softness and manageability are the outcomes people see. Show them in hair, not in adjectives.
3. **Made for professionals.** CHI is tested behind the chair. Stylist technique and education content is brand content.
4. **Cleaner chemistry.** Say plainly what a formula is and is not. Only claim what the specific product supports.

## Writing rules

- **Sentence case** for headlines, buttons and navigation. Capitals are reserved for descriptors set in `descriptor-s` and `descriptor-l`.
- **Second person.** "Your hair", "you". The brand is "we" and "CHI", never "CHI's team of experts".
- **Headlines are four to seven words** and end with a full stop when they are statements.
- **Numbers are numerals**, with units: 450°F / 232°C, 12 fl oz / 355 mL, 1 in / 25 mm.
- **Fahrenheit first, Celsius second** in US materials; Celsius first everywhere else. Always both.
- **No emoji** in packaging, web or print. In social captions, at most one, at the end.
- **No exclamation marks** in headlines.
- Avoid: miracle, magic, flawless, perfect, anti-ageing, chemical-free, "tames" (for textured hair), "fixes".
- Prefer: protects, smooths, strengthens, hydrates, defines, holds, shines.

## Examples

| Context | Write | Not |
|---|---|---|
| Campaign headline | Heat, handled. | Unleash Your Hair's True Potential! |
| Product benefit | Guards hair against heat up to 450°F / 232°C. | Revolutionary thermal shield technology. |
| How to use | Mist over damp hair. Comb through. Style. | For optimal results it is recommended that the product be applied evenly. |
| Stylist content | Take one-inch sections and pass once. | Just glide and go, babe. |
| Error message | We could not add this to your bag. Check your connection and try again. | Oops! Something went wrong. |
| Button | Add to bag | SHOP NOW!!! |

## Naming and trademarks

- Write the brand name as **CHI**, always in capitals, never "Chi" or "chi".
- Use **CHI®** at the first or most prominent mention in a piece, and plain CHI after that.
- Line names follow the master brand: **CHI Infra**, **CHI 44 Iron Guard**. Never drop "CHI" from a line name in a headline.
- Do not make the name possessive or plural: "the CHI range", not "CHI's range".
- Collaborations are written **Partner x CHI** with a lowercase x, as in Barbie x CHI.
- Close any piece that carries the logo with the trademark line set in `legal`.

## Writing for other languages

- CHI, line names and product names stay in Latin script in every market.
- Translate benefits and instructions; do not translate trademarks.
- For scripts Archivo does not cover (Arabic, Cyrillic, Greek, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Thai), set copy in the matching Noto Sans family at the same sizes, with weight 800 standing in for headlines and 400 for copy.
- In right-to-left layouts, mirror the layout but never the logo. The wordmark always reads C, H, I from left to right.
- Leave 30% more room for translated copy than the English needs.
